Verdict

ModelNormal arenasAdversarial arenas (9001–9005: robots must cross)
PPOAmong the best: fast and direct, but its steering wobbles constantly.Fine at open crossings; breaks down at doors and narrow gaps, and crashes into robots without the safety layer.
DQNOn par with PPO but more cautious: slower, stops more, crashes less, wobbles as much.Held up best in 2D; when it fails it gets stuck rather than crashes.
Rule controllerSlightly behind the learned policies; the smoothest, steadiest driver, but gets trapped behind clusters of obstacles.Good in corridors and at the door in 2D, but deadlocks around a central pillar and stalls in clutter.
Tabular QClearly weaker: indirect paths, frequent obstacle hits.Fails almost everywhere except the easy crossroads.
DWA privilegedDecent, very smooth but slow; not deployable (needs the true map).Stalls often, even with the true map.
Go-to-goalBaseline only: crashes whenever something is in the way.Fails almost everywhere.
Caveats: PPO's training cost is from the original project's documentation, not measured in the benchmark. DQN's cost is for the one seed kept; three seeds were trained at the same cost each. Adversarial verdicts rest on one episode per arena, mostly in 2D (Gazebo checks only for PPO): impressions, not measurements.
